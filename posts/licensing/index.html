Why Licensing Matters

Practical Lettering Studio fonts are created by working artists who dedicate time, skill, and research to their craft. This license exists to protect that creative work and to ensure artists are paid fairly for the tools they create.

Licensing fonts supports the continued production of new work. When users purchase a license rather than share or distribute font files, they help preserve the livelihoods of independent designers and keep traditional lettering arts alive.

Your cooperation with these terms allows artists to keep making new typefaces, and ensures you and your clients can use them with confidence and integrity.

Your License

This license is designed to be simple, fair, and perpetual.
• Buy once and use forever.
• Choose your license tier based on your company’s total employee count, including full-time, part-time, consultants, contractors, and freelancers.
• You do not need to upgrade if your company grows. When purchasing new fonts later, select the tier that matches your current size.
• You can use the fonts for any commercial, personal, or non-profit work, including logos, packaging, film/tv/broadcast, and digital design.
• You may not share, resell, or give the font files to others outside your company. Each company or client needs their own license.
• The fonts may be provided to service bureaus solely for output/production of your work, not for their library or future use.
• One license covers everyone in your company. Subsidiaries and separate legal entities need their own license.

Using the fonts

• You are welcome to alter or customize glyphs in your artwork as needed, but the original font files themselves may not be regenerated, converted, embedded, or saved as other formats. The font file may never be included in any software, app, website, or file distribution, whether editable or non-editable.
• You may not create or distribute complete alphabet references, character maps, or glyph catalogs in any format (vector, raster, or otherwise) that would enable others to trace, reference, or reproduce the full typeface. Using individual characters or partial examples within your design work is permitted; this restriction prevents creating reference materials that substitute for purchasing the font.
• Do not change the font names or remove embedded author or license information.
• Never upload the fonts to a server, application, game, or website that allows unlicensed users to access or render the fonts. The artist deserves to be paid when people use the letters.
• The fonts may not be used to create or sell alphabet products: commercial offerings where the font’s letterforms are the product itself, and purchasers can use them to assemble new words or designs.
Examples of prohibited uses:
• Digital embroidery alphabet collections
• Alphabet stencil or stamp sets
• Monogram generators or customization tools
• Letter-by-letter SVG collections for cutting machines
• Alphabet sticker packs or individual letter products
Examples of permitted uses:
• A poster design that happens to show some of the alphabet
• Logo work that includes letterforms
• A children’s book with illustrated letters as part of the narrative
• Educational materials where the alphabet is incidental to the teaching content.

The key distinction: if someone is buying access to the individual letters to create their own words, that’s an alphabet product and is prohibited.

• Never give or send the actual font files to an unlicensed party. If a third party, such as a client or vendor, needs to view or reproduce your design, provide the artwork as a rasterized or outlined file, for example, PDF, JPG, or SVG. The third party must have their own license to install the fonts.

In short: the fonts are yours to use forever. Do not share, embed, or redistribute the font files, create alphabet reference materials, or sell alphabet products.
